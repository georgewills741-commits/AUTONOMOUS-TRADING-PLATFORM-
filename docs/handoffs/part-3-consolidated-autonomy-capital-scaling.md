## 352 — CAPITAL-SCALED SYSTEM CAPABILITY

The platform must intelligently adapt its available capabilities to the actual resources and operating conditions of the account.

The system must NOT treat:

$100

exactly the same operationally as:

$1,000

or:

$10,000

or:

$100,000+

while pretending that the same execution opportunities, liquidity requirements, diversification, infrastructure capacity, or risk capacity exist.

Instead, the system must understand:

```text
CAPITAL
+
LIQUIDITY
+
RISK CAPACITY
+
MARKET CONDITIONS
+
EXECUTION CAPACITY
+
OPERATIONAL READINESS
+
STRATEGY REQUIREMENTS
```

and determine which capabilities are currently appropriate.

This is a capability-scaling system.

## 353 — SMALL CAPITAL MUST REMAIN A FIRST-CLASS OPERATING MODE

A small account must not be treated as an inferior or broken version of the system.

For example:

$100

- Opportunity scanning

- Portfolio management

- AI intelligence where economically justified
- Reconciliation
- Monitoring
- Security
- Auditability
- Capital protection

However, features requiring more capital should not be activated merely because the software technically supports them.

The system should understand what is economically executable.

The platform should not unnecessarily disable useful functionality simply because the account is small.

If a feature can safely operate with small capital, it should remain available.

## 358 — CAPITAL GROWTH MUST NOT AUTOMATICALLY CREATE RECKLESS EXPOSURE

When capital increases:

DO NOT automatically increase:

- Leverage
- Trade frequency
- Position size
- Risk per trade
- Market exposure

unless explicitly authorized and validated.

Capital growth should instead allow the system to evaluate additional opportunities.

## 359 — CAPITAL ALLOCATION SHOULD SCALE INTELLIGENTLY

As capital changes, the Capital Authority should reevaluate:

- Strategy allocations
- Reserve requirements
- Arbitrage reserves
- Venue allocations
- Emergency reserves
- Maximum exposure
- Position sizing
- Opportunity participation
- Capital efficiency

The system should continuously determine whether the existing allocation remains appropriate.

## 360 — CAPITAL EFFICIENCY

The system should consider not only:

"Can I afford this trade?"

but:

"Is allocating this capital here the best authorized use of capital given current opportunities and risk?"

Capital should not remain unnecessarily trapped in low-value allocations.

However, capital should not be constantly moved merely to optimize theoretical utilization.

## 361 — INTELLIGENT REBALANCING — CORRECTED REQUIREMENT

The Rebalancing Engine must be autonomous.

The user should NOT need to manually tell the system:

"Now move funds from Exchange A to Exchange B."

The system must determine when rebalancing is economically and operationally justified.

It should evaluate:

- Current balances
- Reserved balances
- Available balances
- Venue-specific requirements
- Current inventory
- Expected future opportunities
- Historical opportunity frequency
- Expected opportunity value
- Transfer fees
- Blockchain/network fees
- Transfer latency
- Venue liquidity
- Capital utilization
- Reserve requirements
- Risk
- Exchange health
- Withdrawal/deposit restrictions
- Transfer reliability
- Opportunity urgency
- Expected return on transferred capital

## 363 — REBALANCING MUST BE FORWARD-LOOKING

The system should not wait until an opportunity is discovered and then discover that the required capital is on the wrong exchange.

Where economically justified, it should anticipate future capital needs.

Example:

Exchange A currently has excess USDT.

Exchange B has insufficient USDT.

Historical opportunity data indicates Exchange B frequently produces executable opportunities.

The system may determine that moving a portion of capital now is economically justified.

This must be based on measured economics, not arbitrary movement.

## 364 — REBALANCING MUST NOT BECOME CONSTANT CAPITAL CHURN

The system must avoid:

```text
MOVE FUNDS
↓
MOVE THEM BACK
↓
MOVE AGAIN
↓
PAY FEES
↓
LOSE TIME
↓
REDUCE CAPITAL EFFICIENCY
```

The Rebalancing Engine must use:

- Minimum meaningful imbalance thresholds
- Expected opportunity value
- Transfer costs
- Transfer latency
- Reserve requirements
- Hysteresis / anti-churn logic
- Cooldowns where appropriate
- Confidence requirements

## 365 — EMERGENCY REBALANCING

Emergency rebalancing may occur when required to protect operational continuity or satisfy critical risk constraints.

Examples:

- Venue outage
- Unexpected liquidity deterioration
- Critical inventory imbalance
- Capital stranded at unavailable venue
- Risk exposure requiring reduction
- Operational failover
- Critical reserve deficiency

Emergency rebalancing must still pass deterministic authorization and risk controls.

## 366 — REBALANCING IS NOT A SECOND CAPITAL AUTHORITY

The Rebalancing Engine proposes or executes capital movement through the canonical:

CAPITAL AUTHORITY

It must not create a competing capital ledger or independent authority.
