## Conflicts and inconsistencies (CF)

### CF-01 — Order of the capital and risk checks
- **Sources:** §19 (opportunity → capital request → Global Capital Authority → risk check → reservation → execution); §21 (Global Capital Authority → risk/exposure/liquidity → allocation); §94 (strategy → risk → capital → execution); §70 ("capital / risk" as one step).
- **Conflict:** §19/§21 put the capital authority before the risk check; §94 puts risk before capital.
- **Impact:** high. This defines the core pre-trade sequence and the interface between the Risk Engine and the Global Capital Authority.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — [DEC-010](../decisions/DEC-010-pre-trade-decision-flow.md): §19 order is the runtime order (CAP-016).
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** treat §19 as authoritative for the detailed pre-trade sequence (it is the capital-specific flow), and read §94 as a coarse build-dependency example. Needs owner confirmation.

### CF-02 — Where AI sits in the decision flow
- **Sources:** §70 (strategy → AI reasoning → capital/risk); §08 (strategy/risk/capital → "AI only when justified"); §66 (analysis → Trading Director → Devil's Advocate → deterministic Risk Engine); §77 (latency path with no AI step).
- **Conflict:** §08 places AI after risk and capital; §70 and §66 place it before risk.
- **Impact:** medium. Affects whether AI proposals are checked by risk (§66 says risk remains authoritative).
- **Status:** RESOLVED — [DEC-010](../decisions/DEC-010-pre-trade-decision-flow.md): AI, when used, sits before capital and risk; never on arbitrage paths.
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** AI is an optional step that is always upstream of the deterministic Risk Engine (consistent with §26, §66, §70). Read §08 as "AI is invoked only for significant opportunities", not as AI coming after risk.

### CF-03 — Capital reservation missing from the latency-sensitive path
- **Sources:** §77 (market event → … → risk check → execution validation → exchange order); §19 (reservation before capital is committed).
- **Conflict:** the latency path has no capital-reservation step.
- **Impact:** medium. A literal reading of §77 would allow orders without reservation, which violates CAP-003 and CAP-004.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — [DEC-010](../decisions/DEC-010-pre-trade-decision-flow.md): reservation is on the latency path (CAP-021).
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** the latency path includes capital reservation; §77 lists stages, not every mandatory control.

### CF-04 — AI output decision values vs valid outcomes
- **Sources:** §65 (Decision: BUY / SELL / HOLD / NO_TRADE); §27 (valid outcomes include WAIT, REDUCE RISK, SUSPEND STRATEGY, SAFE MODE, ROLLBACK, UNCERTAIN, I DON'T KNOW); §07 (multi-leg routes).
- **Conflict:** the output contract cannot express uncertainty or multi-leg arbitrage decisions, yet §27 forbids manufacturing confidence.
- **Impact:** medium. A schema with no "uncertain" value pushes models toward false decisions.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — [DEC-013](../decisions/DEC-013-ai-organization.md): extended decision values (AIV-013); arbitrage has no AI proposals (ARB-013).
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** extend the conceptual schema so it can express uncertainty, and add a route/leg form for arbitrage, in Part 2's interface contracts.

### CF-05 — Roadmap stage mapping: duplicates and gaps
- **Source:** §95.
- **Mapped twice:** reconciliation (CORE TRADING FOUNDATION and OPERATIONALIZATION); opportunity economics (CORE) vs "true net-profit calculation" (ARBITRAGE).
- **Mapped to one trading stage but shared:** strategy lifecycle, backtesting, and paper trading (DIRECTIONAL, but PAP-002 covers arbitrage); performance controller (ARBITRAGE, but platform-wide per §49).
- **Not mapped to any stage:** Policy System (persistent, versioned policy, §29–§30); operating modes (§31); auditability / event history (§86–§87); system health (§74); model evaluation (§61); structured AI output (§65); AI confidence calibration (§67); numerical precision (§79, cross-cutting); custody/ledger (§84–§85, unconfirmed); security beyond "security foundation" (§89).
- **Impact:** medium. §95 itself says exact sequencing is finalized after dependency analysis.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — [DEC-016](../decisions/DEC-016-roadmap-stage-placement.md): every item placed in exactly one build stage.

### CF-06 — Policy is needed before the stage that delivers it
- **Sources:** §26 (user hard constraints rank second in risk authority); §28–§30 (policy system); §95 (only the NL Policy Interface is mapped, to AI INTELLIGENCE).
- **Conflict:** the Risk Engine (CORE TRADING FOUNDATION) enforces user hard constraints, but structured policy has no stage, and the only policy item is in a later stage.
- **Impact:** high for sequencing.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — [DEC-016](../decisions/DEC-016-roadmap-stage-placement.md): Policy System in CORE TRADING FOUNDATION.
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** place the structured, versioned Policy System (SYS-12) no later than CORE TRADING FOUNDATION, and keep the NL interface (SYS-13) in AI INTELLIGENCE.

### CF-11 — Kill-switch reset: operator-only vs exception-based human intervention
- **Sources:** RSK-009 ([DEC-012](../decisions/DEC-012-safety-architecture.md): only the operator can reset a kill switch) vs OC-1 items 13, 14, 28, 29, 32 (PLT-013 to PLT-015: human intervention by exception; recover automatically when recoverable).
- **Conflict:** kill switches can be activated automatically (for example on exchange instability, API instability, or stale data). With operator-only reset, every such activation stops that part of the platform until a human acts, even after the condition has cleared.
- **Impact:** high for 24/7 operation, and for safety if reset were made fully automatic (e.g. a kill switch triggered by repeated losses, §47).
- **Status:** RESOLVED by the owner — [DEC-021](../decisions/DEC-021-kill-switch-recovery.md): cause-based, risk-aware recovery with escalation (RSK-021 to RSK-025, AUD-008). The owner's answer extends the proposal: more latched causes, a progressive recovery path, and audit.
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** reset by cause.
  - The operator must reset kill switches activated by the operator, by a security event, by unknown financial state, or by loss-based triggers (repeated losses, capital inconsistency).
  - Kill switches activated by recoverable, measurable conditions (venue or API instability, stale data, latency degradation, liquidity collapse) reset automatically once the condition has stayed cleared for a policy-defined period and the recovery checks pass (REC-011).
  - The cause-to-reset mapping lives in policy (POL-011).

### CF-12 — Recovery ordering: verify the database before or after loading state
- **Sources:** REC-007 ([DEC-010](../decisions/DEC-010-pre-trade-decision-flow.md): database integrity verified *before* state is loaded) vs REC-002 (§71: load verified internal state → verify database) and REC-011 (OC-1 item 11: load persistent state → verify database). Also, REC-011 verifies capital, positions, and orders before reconciling with exchange state, while §71 validates capital after reconciliation.
- **Conflict:** ordering only. Every source requires all checks to pass before trading resumes.
- **Impact:** low.
- **Status:** RESOLVED by the owner — [DEC-022](../decisions/DEC-022-restart-recovery-sequence.md): staged recovery; persisted state untrusted until reconciled (REC-014 to REC-018, AUD-009).
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):**
  - No loaded state is relied on until database integrity is verified. This satisfies all three orders, and REC-007 would be reworded to say so.
  - Capital, position, and order checks before reconciliation verify internal consistency; they are validated again against exchange state after reconciliation, as §71 does.

### CF-13 — Lifecycle APPROVAL stage vs automatic canary entry
- **Sources:** STR-001 (§34) and STR-009 (§38): … paper → **approval** → canary → production; vs STR-014 and MODE-007 (OC-1 items 16, 17): automatic canary entry after deterministic "canary authorization"; PLT-014 (OC-1 item 29): human intervention for "production approval gates explicitly designated as human-controlled".
- **Conflict:** the handoff does not say whether APPROVAL is a human decision. If it is, automatic canary entry would bypass it.
- **Impact:** medium. It decides whether any new strategy version reaches live capital without a person.
- **Status:** RESOLVED by the owner — [DEC-023](../decisions/DEC-023-autonomous-canary-approval.md): the Governance and Readiness Engine approves automatically; human approval only where policy requires it; nobody can bypass the gates (STR-019 to STR-022).
- **Proposed resolution (as first written):** the APPROVAL stage is performed by the deterministic canary authorization (STR-013) by default. The operator can designate it human-controlled in policy, globally or per strategy. Owner to confirm, or to state that APPROVAL is always human.

### CF-14 — Policy hierarchy: user hard policy above or below system safety
- **Sources:** P2§101 ("USER HARD POLICY ↓ SYSTEM SAFETY ↓ RISK ↓ CAPITAL ↓ EXECUTION ↓ STRATEGY ↓ AI. No lower layer may override a higher-level hard constraint") and P2§279 ("Hard user constraints outrank lower-level decisions") vs RSK-004 (§26: "system safety → user hard constraints → portfolio / risk policy → validated strategy rules → deterministic market conditions → AI analysis / proposal") and RSK-005.
- **Conflict:** the two orders disagree on whether the user's hard policy or the platform's system-safety rules (RSK-010) come first. While every layer only restricts, the result is the same: all restrictions apply. They differ when a user hard policy would loosen a system-safety rule, for example "trade venue X even while its reconciliation is unresolved". Under RSK-004 that policy is refused. Under P2§101 it could arguably win.
- **Impact:** high. This defines the top of the authority hierarchy that the Risk Engine enforces.
- **Status:** RESOLVED by the owner — [DEC-026](../decisions/DEC-026-safety-floor-and-layered-control.md) ([owner decisions 3](../handoffs/owner-decisions-03-part-2-findings.md), Q1): an immutable safety floor that no AI agent, strategy, policy, administrator, or automatic process can bypass; changing an invariant needs a formal human-controlled change (RSK-034 to RSK-039). RSK-004 is unchanged. The owner's answer goes further than the proposal: a layered model with adaptive operation, recovery, and no deadlock by safety.
- **Proposed resolution — RECOMMENDED, NOT YET APPROVED:** read both as one rule. **System safety is a floor that no policy can lower.** The user's hard policy outranks everything else (risk, capital, execution, strategy, AI) and can always make the platform stricter than system safety requires, but it cannot relax a system-safety rule. The effective restriction is the combination of both. This keeps RSK-004's safety guarantee and gives P2§101 its intent: nothing the platform or AI decides can override the user. If accepted, RSK-004 would get a clarifying note, not new wording, and P2§101 would be recorded as satisfied.

### CF-15 — Environment list: canary and research

### CF-16 — Stage order: the Performance Controller is needed before its stage

### CF-17 — Rule precedence in Part 3: user hard policy above or below system safety
- **Sources:** P3§471 ("USER HARD POLICY ↓ SYSTEM SAFETY ↓ SECURITY ↓ RISK ↓ CAPITAL ↓ EXECUTION ↓ STRATEGY ↓ AI PREFERENCE. No lower layer may override a higher-level hard constraint") vs RSK-004 and the owner's decision on the same question for P2§101 ([DEC-026](../decisions/DEC-026-safety-floor-and-layered-control.md), CF-14).
- **Conflict:** Part 3 repeats Part 2's order, which puts the user's hard policy above system safety, and adds three layers (security, capital, execution) that RSK-004 does not name.
- **Impact:** high: the top of the authority hierarchy.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — decided by the owner on 2026-10-02 ([DEC-035](../decisions/DEC-035-owner-decisions-part-3-findings.md), [owner decisions 4](../handoffs/owner-decisions-04-part-3-findings.md), Q1): security ranks **above** the user's hard constraints, below the safety floor. RSK-048 (the builder's placement, which put security below) is replaced by RSK-049. The builder's first resolution ([DEC-031](../decisions/DEC-031-part-3-reconciliation.md)) applied DEC-026 (the safety floor stays on top; RSK-004 unchanged) and placed Part 3's new layers in Part 3's own order (RSK-048); the consequence put to the owner is below.
- **Consequence put to the owner (answered in DEC-035):** in RSK-048, as in P3§471, security sits below the user's hard constraints. Security rules inside the safety floor stay above everything (for example RSK-010: trading credentials cannot withdraw). Other security controls rank below the user's hard policy, for example the transfer-authority restrictions (SEC-006, SEC-007) and AI least privilege (SEC-008). This is the same kind of question as CF-14: should security controls outside the floor rank above the user's hard policy instead?

### CF-18 — Capital growth and automatic scaling of limits
- **Sources:** P3§358 (do not automatically increase leverage, trade frequency, position size, risk per trade, or market exposure when capital grows "unless explicitly authorized and validated") and P3§441 (realized P&L → available capital → authorized capital) vs RSK-036 (the platform may adjust limits and allocation to available capital, [DEC-026](../decisions/DEC-026-safety-floor-and-layered-control.md)), CAP-030 and CAP-032 (buckets and capabilities scale with capital, [DEC-028](../decisions/DEC-028-capital-buckets-and-progressive-activation.md)), and POL-005 (raising authorized capital, leverage, or venues is an important policy change).
- **Conflict:** read alone, P3§358 forbids what RSK-036 allows, and P3§441 raises authorized capital automatically where POL-005 requires the operator.
- **Impact:** high: how the platform's exposure grows with its capital.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-02 ([DEC-035](../decisions/DEC-035-owner-decisions-part-3-findings.md), [owner decisions 4](../handoffs/owner-decisions-04-part-3-findings.md), Q2). The resolution below stands unchanged: [DEC-031](../decisions/DEC-031-part-3-reconciliation.md) (builder; all requirements kept): growth updates available capital and the capability assessment automatically; it raises those limits or authorized capital only where the operator's policy already authorizes that scaling and the Readiness System has validated it; anything else is a POL-005 change.

### CF-19 — Feature lifecycle state model
- **Sources:** master execution constitution §107 ("Every major feature must have a lifecycle": IDEA, PROPOSED, APPROVED, DESIGNED, IMPLEMENTING, IMPLEMENTED, VERIFYING, PAPER/SAFE TEST, CANARY, PRODUCTION, DEPRECATED, RETIRED; "The exact state model must be canonical") and §109 (every feature has a known status), [DEC-033](../decisions/DEC-033-adopt-master-execution-constitution.md), vs GOV-018 (P3§509, PROPOSED: the repository "may" keep features with the statuses PROPOSED, RESEARCH, APPROVED, IN DEVELOPMENT, VALIDATING, DEPLOYED, DEPRECATED, RETIRED) and GOV-009 (ACTIVE, DEPRECATED, SUNSET_PENDING, RETIRED, REPLACED).
- **Conflict:** two different state lists for the same thing. §107 requires one canonical model; Part 3 only proposes one. The names differ: IN DEVELOPMENT vs IMPLEMENTING, VALIDATING vs VERIFYING, DEPLOYED vs PRODUCTION; RESEARCH has no counterpart in §107; IDEA, DESIGNED, IMPLEMENTED, PAPER/SAFE TEST, and CANARY have none in P3§509.
- **Impact:** medium: the feature status every capability carries (§109), the capability registry (RDY-020), and GOV-018's class. Nothing is blocked before implementation starts.
- **Proposed resolution:** RECOMMENDED — NOT YET APPROVED. Adopt §107's states as the canonical feature lifecycle: it is the owner's newer instruction and the only one that says "must". Map P3§509 onto it (IN DEVELOPMENT = IMPLEMENTING; VALIDATING = VERIFYING; DEPLOYED = CANARY or PRODUCTION; RESEARCH = the evaluation between IDEA and PROPOSED); keep GOV-009's SUNSET_PENDING and REPLACED as statuses within DEPRECATED and RETIRED; record the choice and GOV-018's new class in a decision record. Strategies keep their own lifecycle (STR-024), which RDY-007 already separates from readiness.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — decided by the owner on 2026-10-02 ([DEC-035](../decisions/DEC-035-owner-decisions-part-3-findings.md), [owner decisions 4](../handoffs/owner-decisions-04-part-3-findings.md), Q3): the option shown to the owner, "Constitution's lifecycle": §107's states, with Part 3's names mapped onto them and strategies keeping their own lifecycle. GOV-024 is the canonical feature lifecycle; GOV-018 is replaced. The GOV-009 part of the proposed resolution was not in that option; it stays a builder reading ([Architecture governance](../architecture/architecture-governance.md), "Status vocabularies").

### CF-20 — Local hosting vs a production platform that does not depend on the owner's computer
- **Sources:** MIG-001 ("The platform must support local hosting and remote server / cloud hosting. The user must not be forced into one hosting architecture"), MIG-002 (possible local components, local execution among them), and PLT-017 ("local hosting" among the platform's capabilities) vs the owner's master knowledge-base audit of 2026-10-02, section 16 ("The production platform must NOT depend on: … the user's laptop"; the request is quoted in full in the [audit record](../traceability/master-knowledge-base-audit-2026-10-02.md)). Related, and on the request's side: MIG-004 ("Trading components must not assume 'this service always runs on the user's PC.'"), GOV-023 (the platform stays operable if "the original development environment changes"), high availability with an active and a standby host (REC-021, REC-023, [DEC-030](../decisions/DEC-030-high-availability-and-single-active-copy.md)), and rebuilding production without the owner's physical availability (OPS-017).
- **Conflict (possibly only an ambiguity):** three readings. (1) Production must never run on the owner's own computer: this narrows MIG-001's local hosting for production. (2) Production must not depend on any single machine, the owner's included: DEC-030, OPS-017, and MIG-004 already meet it, and local hosting stays a supported option. (3) In the request's own context (section 16 separates Claude Code from the platform), "the user's laptop" is the machine the builder works on: then there is no conflict, and GOV-023 and PLT-029 already meet it.
- **Impact:** medium: the hosting design and the production-readiness model (OPERATIONALIZATION). The hosting abstraction and portable configuration (MIG-004, MIG-010), which apply from the first implemented stage, are the same either way. Nothing in Stage 1 is blocked.
- **Proposed resolution:** RECOMMENDED — NOT YET APPROVED. Reading (2), which also covers reading (3): production must not depend on any single machine, the owner's computer included (an active and a standby host, DEC-030; rebuilt without the owner, OPS-017). Local hosting (MIG-001, MIG-002) stays supported, for production only on hosts that meet the same production-readiness criteria. The owner confirms this, or says that production must never run on the owner's own computer.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — decided by the owner on 2026-10-03 ([DEC-036](../decisions/DEC-036-owner-decisions-audit-findings.md), [owner decisions 5](../handoffs/owner-decisions-05-audit-findings.md), Q1): reading (2), as recommended. Production never depends on any one machine, the owner's computer included; a machine of the owner's may host production only if it meets the same readiness bar as a server. MIG-033 added; MIG-001 and MIG-002 unchanged.

### CF-21 — PLT-010's wording on withdrawals vs DEC-006 and SEC-006
- **Sources:** PLT-010 ([DEC-006](../decisions/DEC-006-single-operator-and-trading-ledger.md)): "It does not take custody of funds and has no deposit, withdrawal, or multi-user account functions" vs DEC-006's own decision text ("There is no custody, no deposit or withdrawal handling for others, and no multi-user accounts") and SEC-006 ([DEC-019](../decisions/DEC-019-company-grade-autonomous-operating-model.md), from OC-1 item 4): a separate "rebalancing transfer authority (approved transfers between approved exchange accounts)"; "The platform itself holds no general withdrawal or custody authority".
- **Conflict:** PLT-010 drops DEC-006's "for others". Read literally, it forbids any withdrawal function, including the rebalancing transfers between the operator's own venue accounts that DEC-019 approved. DEC-019's reconciliation notes read the two as consistent (rebalancing between the operator's own accounts is not custody).
- **Impact:** low: wording only; the transfer design follows SEC-006 and SEC-007.
- **Proposed resolution:** RECOMMENDED — NOT YET APPROVED. Bring PLT-010's wording in line with DEC-006 ("no deposit or withdrawal handling for others") and point it to SEC-006. This changes a requirement's text, so it needs a decision record. Until then PLT-010 is read through DEC-019's note and SEC-006.
- **Status:** RESOLVED — found by the Gate 3 review of the [master knowledge-base audit](../traceability/master-knowledge-base-audit-2026-10-02.md); decided by the owner on 2026-10-03 ([DEC-036](../decisions/DEC-036-owner-decisions-audit-findings.md), [owner decisions 5](../handoffs/owner-decisions-05-audit-findings.md), Q3), as recommended: PLT-010 reworded to match DEC-006 and to name SEC-006 and SEC-007.
